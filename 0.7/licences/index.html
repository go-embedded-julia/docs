Licences

Julia is MIT and may be translated to Go; GMP and MPFR are replaced, not linked; the GPL parts of SuiteSparse are avoided.

You are reading the documentation of an outdated version, whose text may no longer be accurate. Go to the latest version.

Status: planned. No code has been translated yet. This page records what the plan allows, and the rules it will hold to.

What may be translated to Go, with the notice kept

Julia is MIT, and Julia’s THIRDPARTY.md states that the language “consists of the compiler (the contents of src/), most of the standard library (base/ and stdlib/)”. So these are all MIT and may be translated to Go with the notice kept:

SourceLicence
src/ (the C runtime)MIT
base/, stdlib/MIT
JuliaSyntax/MIT
JuliaLowering/MIT
JuliaInterpreter.jlMIT
LinearAlgebra.jl, Statistics.jlMIT
src/flisp (femtolisp)BSD-3-Clause
dSFMTBSD-3-Clause; may be translated

Inside Base, with their own licence

base/ryu/* is under the Boost licence, and base/special/rem_pio2.jl and base/special/hyperbolic.jl carry an FDLIBM notice. Both run as Julia through the interpreter, and the notices travel with the image.

The GPL parts to avoid

SuiteSparse’s libumfpack, libspqr and librbio are GPL-2.0-or-later, and libcholmod is LGPL-2.1-or-later and GPL-2.0-or-later. They back SparseArrays, an external standard library, whose \ on sparse matrices goes to UMFPACK and CHOLMOD. A Go port of SparseArrays must use the fleet’s sparse solvers (go-sparse) and must not read SuiteSparse source. Julia’s stock build includes these libraries (USE_GPL_LIBS ?= 1 in Make.inc), so they are a thing to keep out, not a thing that is absent.

FFTW is GPL but is not in Julia’s tree: FFTW.jl is a registry package, and the plan serves its API over go-fft instead.
